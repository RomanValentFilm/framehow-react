  • country derived from network information;

  • project activity;
  • storage usage;

  • error and security information; and

4. Project Content and Other People's Personal Data

Framehow users decide what material they upload.

For professional or organisational use, you or your organisation may be the controller of personal data contained in your project while Framehow acts as a processor providing technical storage and processing.

Where required, this relationship may be governed by a separate Framehow Data Processing Addendum.

5. Confidentiality and Security

Projects and uploaded content are private by default.

Framehow uses reasonable technical and organisational measures to protect stored content against unauthorised access, disclosure, alteration or loss.

Private project content is not publicly accessible and is not used for advertising or general AI training.

Access by authorised Framehow personnel is limited to circumstances reasonably necessary to operate, secure, maintain or support the Service, or where required by law.

Framehow currently uses HTTPS/TLS for production traffic. Stored project data is hosted using Cloudflare infrastructure.

Stored content is encrypted in transit and on Cloudflare's storage, but Framehow does not provide end-to-end encryption. This means the content is not locked with a key that only you hold, and authorised administrators of Framehow or its infrastructure could technically access it where necessary.

6. AI and Automated Processing

Framehow does not currently send project images, text or other User Content to generative AI services such as Anthropic, OpenAI or Google for processing or training.

PDF processing and storyboard extraction take place in the user's browser.

Where OCR is required to read text from a PDF, Framehow currently uses Tesseract.js, which performs the image recognition locally on the user's device.

The application itself has been developed using development tools that may include AI-assisted coding tools. This does not mean production User Content is sent to those tools.

7. Storage and Image Processing

Each account currently has 150 MB of storage until the account email address is confirmed, and 500 MB after the email address is confirmed.

Individual uploaded images are currently limited to 10 MB per image.

An account can currently hold up to 50 projects, and each project can currently hold up to 300 shots.

These limits are shown in the app and may change as Framehow develops.

Pictures captured through Framehow, and pictures added from a device by loading, folder import, or drag and drop, are resized to at most approximately 2400 pixels on the long side and re-encoded before storage.

Re-encoding removes embedded image metadata such as EXIF data and GPS location information before storage.

Project information is stored using Cloudflare infrastructure and may also remain locally on devices used to access Framehow.

Framehow's R2 file storage is located in Cloudflare's Eastern Europe region (EEUR), selected automatically by Cloudflare. The D1 database uses Cloudflare's automatic location selection. No EU-only jurisdiction restriction is configured.

Cloudflare may process data in other countries under its standard data-transfer safeguards, including Standard Contractual Clauses and, where applicable, the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework.

PDF files imported into Framehow are currently processed locally in the browser and are not uploaded to Framehow's server as PDF files. Extracted images may subsequently be stored as part of the project.

8. Local Device Storage

Framehow stores certain information locally on the user's device so that the app can function, synchronise and support offline work.

This may include account/session information, the currently open project, unsynchronised project changes, temporary local project copies, image previews, preferences and application cache data.

Some local copies may remain temporarily on a device even after logout.

Users can normally remove local application data using their browser or device storage controls.

Device permissions

The iPhone and iPad app may ask for access to the camera (to take pictures for a shot), the photo library (to pick pictures you choose), and Files (to select storyboards, PDFs or other supported files you choose). It may also ask for permission to add pictures to Photos when you save exported images.

Framehow only receives the pictures or files you actively take, pick or select. It does not read the rest of your photo library or Files, and it does not use the microphone or location.

9. Deletion and Retention

Deleted projects

A normally deleted project remains recoverable for approximately 7 days.

It is then permanently deleted from Framehow's active systems.

Choosing "Delete now" permanently deletes it immediately.

Deleted shots, versions and replaced pictures

These disappear from the active project immediately.

Earlier pictures may remain referenced by automatic restore points for up to approximately 48 hours.

A restore point deliberately saved by the user may remain until the user deletes it.

Deleted library pictures

Pictures deleted directly from the Framehow library are permanently removed and cannot be restored through Framehow.

Deleted accounts

When you delete your account, your active sessions are ended and your account is retained for approximately 7 days so that it can be restored by signing in again.

After that period, the account, associated projects and pictures are permanently deleted from Framehow's active systems.

Inactive accounts

Framehow may delete an account, together with all of its projects and pictures, if the account has not been used for 12 months.

Before deleting an inactive account, Framehow will send a warning to the account email address at least 30 days in advance.

Signing in during that warning period keeps the account active and prevents that inactivity deletion.

Infrastructure retention

Framehow operates no separate user backup system.

Cloudflare may maintain infrastructure-level redundancy, technical logs or database recovery mechanisms for limited periods as part of operating its services. Object-level access logging for Framehow file storage is switched off. These mechanisms are not user-accessible Framehow backups.

Other Cloudflare technical logs may remain for a limited period under Cloudflare's own policies, including after application-level deletion.

10. Analytics

Framehow currently uses its own first-party analytics to understand how the Service is being used.

These first-party analytics may include information such as feature or screen used, device category, browser family, whether Framehow is used in a browser, as an installed web app, or as the App Store app, country, session identifier, user identifier when signed in, and limited project-related metadata such as project name. Framehow does not store the user's IP address in this analytics data; only the country derived from network information is stored.

When a user is signed in, analytics events may be linked to that user account. Analytics data linked to an account is deleted when the account is deleted. All other analytics data is retained for up to 12 months.

Framehow does not currently use Google Analytics, Meta Pixel, Hotjar, PostHog or similar third-party behavioural analytics tools.

Framehow may introduce additional analytics tools, including third-party tools, in the future.

11. Service Providers

Framehow relies on service providers to operate the Service.

Cloudflare — used for application hosting, database, image/file storage, email delivery, DNS, TLS and related technical infrastructure.

jsDelivr and Tesseract-related CDN services — used to load software components and language data used for browser-based OCR. These providers receive normal network information such as IP address, but Framehow does not send project images to them for OCR processing.

Apple — where Framehow is distributed through Apple's App Store or TestFlight, Apple processes information under its own terms and privacy arrangements.

We do not sell personal data.

12. International Data Transfers

Some service providers may process information outside the European Economic Area.

Where required, transfers are made using an appropriate legal mechanism, such as an adequacy decision, the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework or Standard Contractual Clauses.

Framehow's R2 file storage is located in Cloudflare's Eastern Europe region (EEUR), selected automatically by Cloudflare. The D1 database also uses Cloudflare's automatic location selection. Framehow has not configured an EU-only jurisdiction restriction. Cloudflare may therefore process data in other countries under its standard safeguards, including Standard Contractual Clauses and, where applicable, the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework.

13. Cookies and Similar Storage

Framehow currently does not use ordinary user authentication cookies.

Instead, account sessions and offline functionality use browser storage such as localStorage, IndexedDB, session storage and application caching.

These technologies are used for functions including keeping the user signed in, storing unsynchronised work, offline functionality, project synchronisation, device identification, preferences and first-party analytics.

If Framehow later introduces non-essential marketing or third-party tracking technologies, any consent required by applicable law will be requested before they are activated.
